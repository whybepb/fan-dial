Why a dial

macOS waits until
your chip is already hot

The automatic fan curve is tuned to stay quiet. During a long build, a render, or a game on a warm afternoon, it lets the chip climb first and ramps later. FanDial lets you spin the fans up before the heavy work starts.

The automatic curve

It reacts to heat that is already there. On a long job, the fans often catch up after the chip has started to throttle.

The dial

You choose the RPM before the job begins. When you are done, one tap returns the fans to the automatic curve.

In the menu bar

Everything you need,
nothing that fights macOS

Set it in one drag

The target applies when you let go. It stays inside the range the SMC reports, 2317 to 7826 rpm on an M4 Pro.

Auto is one tap away

Tap the center or the Auto pill. Quit FanDial and the fans go back to macOS without you doing anything.

Your setting survives sleep

Wake resets the firmware unlock. With the helper installed, FanDial notices and applies your RPM again.

One password prompt, then none

Install the optional helper once and later changes apply silently. Skip it and macOS asks on each change.

Temperatures under the dial

CPU and GPU die sensors, averaged, so you can see what the fans are doing for you.

A CLI if you prefer the terminal

fanctl reads fans and temps with no root, and sets them with it.

$ ./fanctl status $ sudo ./fanctl set 4000 $ sudo ./fanctl auto

How it works

From clone to cool
in three steps

  1. 1

    Build it

    No Xcode project. The script needs the Swift compiler from Xcode or the Command Line Tools, and macOS 14 or later.

    git clone https://github.com/whybepb/fan-dial cd fan-dial && ./build.sh open FanDial.app

  2. 2

    Drag the ring

    A fan icon appears in the menu bar. Click it and drag the ring. The first change takes a few seconds while FanDial unlocks the fan keys. The icon fills while you are in manual.

  3. 3

    Tap to hand back

    Tap the center or press Auto. macOS takes over its normal curve in about 10 ms. Press Max when you want everything the fans have.

How it gets past thermalmonitord

From the M3 generation on, macOS holds the fan keys and the firmware rejects writes with SMC result 0x82. FanDial uses the unlock documented by agoodkind/macos-smc-fan and implemented in raminsharifi/MacFanControl. On M5 the mode key is lowercase with no gate, and FanDial handles both.

  1. Try F0Md = 1. On 0x82, write the diagnostic flag Ftst = 1 and wait about 3 s.
  2. Retry the mode write every 100 ms. It yields in about 3 to 6 s, longer under load.
  3. Write the target to F0Tg. Keep Ftst = 1 while manual.
  4. To release, set F0Md = 0 per fan, then Ftst = 0, so thermalmonitord can reclaim the fans.

FAQ

Questions before you build

Which Macs does it work on?

FanDial is built for Apple Silicon and tested on a MacBook Pro with an M4 Pro. It handles the M3 and M4 unlock and the lowercase mode key on M5. It needs macOS 14 or later. It has not been tested on Intel Macs.

Is it safe for my Mac?

Targets are clamped to the minimum and maximum your Mac's SMC reports, so the dial cannot ask for a speed the firmware does not allow. Its floor is the SMC minimum, not zero. Setting fans low under heavy load makes the chip throttle sooner. FanDial writes through a diagnostic firmware path, so you use it at your own risk.

Why does the first change take a few seconds?

On M3 and later, thermalmonitord holds the fan keys. FanDial sets a diagnostic flag and retries until the firmware yields. On the M4 Pro that takes 1 to 4 s. After that, changes take about 0.15 s.

Why does it ask for my password?

Reading fan speeds needs no privileges. Writing a target needs root, so macOS shows its own admin dialog. FanDial never sees your password or your Touch ID.

Can I stop the password prompts?

Yes. Click "Stop asking for my password" in the popover and approve once. That installs fanctl as a setuid helper that only accepts status, set, max and auto. Remove it any time with sudo rm /usr/local/bin/fanctl.

Can I run it next to Macs Fan Control?

No. Two apps writing the same fan target give confusing results. Quit one of them before you use the other.

What happens when my Mac sleeps?

Wake resets the unlock flag. With the helper installed, FanDial applies your RPM again. If macOS keeps reclaiming the fans, FanDial backs off and returns to auto.

What happens when I quit?

FanDial hands the fans back to macOS automatically. You do not need to press Auto first.

Is there a ready made download?

Not yet. You build it from source with ./build.sh, which takes one command and produces FanDial.app and fanctl.

What does it cost?

Nothing. FanDial is MIT licensed. Read the code, fork it, change it.
